Some water problems need a phone call this minute, and others can safely wait until morning. We have ordered these five common Sarasota calls by how fast each one needs attention.

Bursts: A pipe that has split is the most urgent call there is, because it puts out water for as long as the supply is on. Depending on the season, the break may follow a freeze, and it often shows only when the ice inside melts. Shut the house supply, run a tap to drain the lines and phone us immediately, whatever the hour. If a ceiling is sagging from the weight of water, stay out from under it and do not poke it.

Runoff: Storm water coming under a door or through a garage is next in line. You cannot stop the rain, but you can move belongings up and keep away from any outlets near the water. Having passed over pavement and soil on the way in, it carries dirt and germs. As soon as it is safe to enter, we pump the water out and take away porous items it ruined.

Kitchens: Leaks from a dishwasher, an ice maker line or the plumbing under the kitchen sink should be dealt with the day you find them. Close the small valve under the sink or behind the appliance and the flow usually stops. What remains is the water trapped below the cabinets and floor covering, and it will not dry by itself. In Sarasota kitchens we often dry cabinets in place by sending air through small holes in the toe kick.

Crawlspaces: Moisture under a raised floor is seldom an emergency, but it should not be put off for a season. Drying Out a Sarasota Property: What to Expect

A wet building left alone does dry eventually, but far too slowly. Left alone, water trapped in drywall, wood framing and subfloor can stay for weeks, which is long enough for mold and warping. Our job on every Sarasota property is to speed that up in a controlled way.

Extraction is always step one, since pumping and vacuuming remove water much faster than any drying machine. Then air movers are set along the walls to push a fast stream of air across wet surfaces and lift moisture out of them. That vapor is collected by dehumidifiers and pumped away, keeping the air dry so the process does not stall. Without the dehumidifiers, the air movers would just move damp air around.

We take moisture readings every day and compare them with a dry part of the same building. Most homes in Sarasota reach that dry standard in 3 to 5 days, with dense materials like hardwood or plaster taking longer. We remove the machines when the readings reach the goal, and not before.

How Sarasota Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

Houses tell you when water is getting where it should not. The signs are easy to miss if you do not know them, so here is what our technicians look for in Sarasota homes. Paint that bubbles or peels, a brown ring on a ceiling, baseboards pulling away from the wall, and flooring that cups or lifts all mean moisture behind the surface. A musty smell in a closet, a toilet that rocks, or a tap that drips belongs on the list too.

None of these is an emergency on its own, but each is cheaper to fix now than later. Re-caulk tubs and showers when the bead cracks, since water behind tile does damage you will not see for months. Test a sump pump several times a year by filling the pit until the float trips and the motor runs. Keep stored items off the basement or garage floor on shelves or in plastic bins.

Good upkeep lowers the odds, but it cannot remove them. Fewer and smaller leaks are still a real gain, especially compared with a Sarasota home full of drying machines for most of a week.
